**To:** sales@themoviedb.org
**Subject:** Licence question: Movie Match, a free film recommendation app (web and Android)

Hello,

I am [____ name], an independent developer in [____ country]. I am building Movie Match,
a free film recommendation service that uses the TMDB API. Before its public web launch I
would like to confirm which terms apply to it.

**What it does.** People rate films they have seen. Movie Match suggests other films,
shows how closely each one matches their taste, and gives a short reason. There is a
website ([____ website]) and an Android app.

**Where it is now:**
- A closed test with about [____ 7] invited testers runs from 2026-11-29 to 2026-12-13.
  The testers get the Android app as an APK file directly from me; it is not on Google
  Play.
- The public web launch is planned for 2026-12-15. A Google Play release has no date yet.
- I expect about [____] monthly active users in the first months.

**Money.** The service is free: no ads, no subscriptions, no paid features, no
sponsorship and no affiliate links. Nothing about it earns revenue, and none is planned
for the launch. If that ever changes, I will contact you first and sign whatever licence
is needed before any revenue feature ships.

**How it uses TMDB:**
- **Metadata:** title, overview, release date, runtime, genres, keywords, and director
  and cast names. About 5,000 films are stored; 500 of them are used for recommendations.
  A background job fetches and refreshes them, never inside a user's request.
- **Images:** posters and backdrops load from image.tmdb.org at TMDB's standard sizes.
  I do not store or redistribute the image files.
- **Derived data:** for each of the 500 films I computed, once, my own scores on 14 taste
  dimensions (for example pacing or humour). To do this, each film's title, year,
  runtime, genres, keywords, director and overview were sent to an AI model (Google's
  Gemini API). I also computed, once, a numeric embedding of each film's text with
  Google's embedding model, to find similar films. Only the scores and the embeddings are
  stored and used; they are not published as a dataset.
- **No AI at launch:** the public launch calls no AI model from user requests. During the
  closed test, a short "why you may like it" sentence was generated by the same AI model
  from a film's scores and a tester's taste; those stored sentences may still be shown.
- **Attribution:** the notice "This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or
  certified by TMDB" and a link to themoviedb.org are on the About screen, on the web and
  in the Android app. The TMDB logo is being added there before the public launch.
- **Not used:** watch-provider (JustWatch) data, and TMDB user or account data.

My questions:
1. Is a free public launch as described — no ads and no revenue of any kind — covered by
   the non-commercial terms of use, or does it need a commercial licence?
2. Was computing my own scores and embeddings by sending film metadata to an AI model,
   and storing them, within your terms? If not, what should I change (for example, delete
   them, or compute them differently)?
3. Is distributing the Android app as an APK to testers, outside Google Play, fine under
   the same terms?
4. Is there anything else in this use I should change before the launch?

Thank you for your time. I am happy to give more details or a demo.
